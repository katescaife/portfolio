Case study · Adoption & change management

Turning a platform nobody used into 100%+ adoption

Global field org · multi-channel rollout · 100%+ increase in usage

The situation

Splunk had recently switched the field from its old content management system to a new content platform with real capabilities the old one didn't have: sharing tools and analytics tied to every customer share. These were robust, actionable insights the field wasn't taking advantage of. The instinct was that it just needed more training, but I took a different approach: an adoption and change management program, not a training push.

The diagnosis

The field kept using the new platform exactly like the old one: downloading assets to their computers and sharing over email, missing the sharing and analytics tools entirely and risking sending outdated content in the process. Some sellers knew the new capabilities existed, but most hadn't connected them to any real value. The fix wasn't more how-to content. It was giving the field a bit of the how, with a heavy dose of why.

Design decisions

  • A "Battle of the Sellers" video. A short, high-quality piece styled like a fictional game show: two sellers racing to close first, one using the platform's sharing and analytics tools, one not. It played at a company-wide sales town hall and became a permanent part of new-hire onboarding's tools segment.
  • Three purpose-built walkthroughs live on the platform. A "top shareable assets" walkthrough, built with product marketing and refreshed monthly, surfaced the most deal-moving content right on the homepage. A second prompted anyone sharing without a customer signature configured to set one up. A third offered general how-to guidance on demand.
  • A role-based roadshow. My team and I got 15–20 minutes on regional leadership calls across the globe, tailoring the use cases to the audience, sales versus tech sellers, with small activities and awards for the winners.
  • A recurring "user of the month" spotlight, making adoption visible and socially rewarded rather than a compliance checkbox.
  • An end-of-call reminder slide on virtual enablement sessions: one slide, unobtrusive, surfacing the top shareable asset tied to that specific session's content.

Result

A 100%+ increase in usage of the platform's actual capabilities. No single tactic carried it: a piece of content that made the why memorable, always-on nudges built into the platform itself, a live roadshow that reached the field directly, and small reminders landing at the exact moments usage mattered.

Awareness, motivation, and a trigger at the moment of relevance. None of those three alone would have closed the gap — it took building the whole system.

Reflection

This is my clearest example of taking a broad, open-ended mandate and building the full system that closed it: content, platform-level nudges, live reach, and recurring reinforcement, rather than running one campaign and hoping it landed.
