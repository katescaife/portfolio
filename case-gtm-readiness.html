Case study · Process design

Getting one team to a shared definition of "ready"

Cross-functional GTM process · company-wide adoption in one cycle

The situation

At Solvvy, there was already a process for getting product requirements onto the roadmap, and one person's Notion database tracking products, versions, compatibility, and capabilities. What it didn't have was a shared definition of how ready a release needed to be before it shipped, or who owed what and by when. GTM readiness varied release to release, and there was no clear, cross-functional vision of what a successful launch actually looked like.

Design decisions

  • Got the org aligned on defined priority tiers and the specific deliverables owed at each tier. This was the real unlock: a shared definition of "ready," not a new tool or a new team.
  • Built that tiering on top of the existing Notion database instead of replacing it, and folded it into the existing product-requirements process rather than standing up a parallel one, preserving the institutional knowledge already living there.
  • Secured agreement to a code freeze ahead of every release — freezing several days before a scheduled ship date — so I could get into the finished product early enough to build accurate screenshots, videos, and usage notes before launch, instead of scrambling after the fact.

Result

One coordinated release motion: product, internal enablement, external documentation, and product marketing materials all launched the same day, with internal training often landing a day or two ahead of the rest. Adopted company-wide within a single cycle.

Reflection

This is a clean example of getting an existing, half-working system to actually work by defining what "ready" meant and negotiating the access to prove it, rather than asking the org to learn something new.
