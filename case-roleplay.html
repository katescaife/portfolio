Case study · AI-powered learning experience design

Building the field's AI roleplay practice system

9 roleplays · 377 unique learners · 4.3/5 across 142 ratings

The situation

MindTickle shipped AI-powered roleplay as a new platform capability. Leadership wanted to see it actually work for sellers, at scale, globally, but nobody yet knew what "good" looked like for this format.

Design decisions

  • Built a meta-prompt system: prompts that build the prompts. A single roleplay needs far more than a customer name and a scenario, it needs the full layer of detail that makes it feel authentic, and building that by hand, at volume, with enough variation while keeping everything aligned, doesn't scale. The meta-prompt system generated that detail consistently, and also enforced a consistent structure across every roleplay, since the structure and format of the prompt itself mattered a lot to how well the roleplay actually played out.
  • Iterated through test groups until the format held up. Scalability wasn't assumed, it was tested and adjusted before rolling out broadly.
  • Designed the full learner experience around the roleplay itself, not just the prompt. The lead-in, the instructions, and optional reference handouts learners could use during the interaction meant learners effectively chose their own difficulty: lean on the scaffolding for easier practice, or skip it to test recall cold. Everything was oriented around practicing one thing well: a good customer call.
  • Built the standard rubric the AI grader uses to evaluate every interaction. Turned a novel format into something with a consistent, defensible evaluation standard rather than a black box.
  • Created supplementary content to reinforce the series, short video pieces featuring peers delivering pitches and customer stories, plus relevant decks, learning tools, and discovery guides tied to that scenario.

Result

In its first measured stretch: 9 roleplays in the series, 377 unique learners, 722 engaged attempts, 537 completions, and 142 ratings averaging 4.3 out of 5.

"The AI was super valuable and a great way to practice talking about a new subject to a customer!" — one of 142 ratings, averaging 4.3 out of 5

Learner feedback also included: "This is an extremely cool adaptation of AI for training purposes." The supplementary content held up independently too: the highest-viewed piece pulled 590 views, 120 of them from learners who found it on their own, not funneled in from a roleplay.

Where it's headed

I've since adapted this approach into a broader AI Coach concept, a three-mode system that explains a topic, tests understanding through a live roleplay, then delivers direct feedback, planned for launch as part of the Q2 QEP.
